Why the questions

0707

A quote against tonnage alone is a guess

Two 20 T cranes can differ by a factor of two in price. The tonnage is the one number that changes least.

Girder depth grows faster than span because deflection, not stress, usually governs the section. Adding four metres of span to a 20 T crane costs more than adding five tonnes of capacity to it. This is the single most common reason two quotations disagree.

Shifts per day and lifts per hour set the class under IS 3177 and FEM 9.511, and the class sets the hoist group, gearbox rating, brake selection and wheel material. A crane bought at M5 duty and worked at M7 fails early, and that failure is not a warranty claim.

A ladle crane is designed under IS 4137 with redundant braking on the hoist, thermal shielding, and a mechanism selected for the consequence of a dropped load rather than for its likelihood. That is why the hot-metal question is asked first and why it cannot be answered later.

If a runway already exists, the wheel loads of the new crane have to be checked against it. If it does not, columns, gantry girders, rails and stops are part of the supply and often cost more than the crane. A quotation that does not say which case it assumes cannot be compared with one that does.

Design standards and wind loads

Outdoor gantry crane design

Gantry cranes for outdoor service are designed with wind load calculations per IS 875 (Part 3), rail clamps and storm anchors, weather-protected panels, and hot-dip galvanized or high-build epoxy finish. Legs are designed for rail-wheel loading, ground settlement, and lateral forces from wind and load swing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a gantry crane and a goliath crane?

A gantry crane is a portal crane where the bridge is supported on legs that travel on ground-level rails. A goliath crane is a full-span gantry where both legs are the same height. A semi-goliath has one leg on the ground rail and the other on an elevated runway. The terms are often used interchangeably.

When should I choose a gantry crane instead of an EOT crane?

When you do not have a building structure to hang the runway from, or when the load moves in an outdoor yard or open area. Gantry cranes are standard for precast plants, steel stockyards, shipyards, container yards, and construction sites.

Can gantry cranes work outdoors in all weather?

Yes, when specified with rail clamps, storm anchors, weather canopies, IP-rated panels, and hot-dip galvanized or high-build epoxy paint system. SVIND designs outdoor gantry cranes with wind load calculations per IS 875 and provisions for monsoon and storm conditions.

What foundation work is needed for a gantry crane?

Ground rails mounted on a continuous concrete foundation with anchor bolts, designed for wheel loads, ground settlement, and lateral forces. SVIND provides wheel load calculations and foundation drawings for your civil consultant. [CLIENT TO CONFIRM — typical foundation depth and concrete grade requirements.]

How is the rail gauge decided?

Rail gauge (distance between the two ground rails) is set by the span you need to cover, plus clearances for the legs and load swing. SVIND calculates this based on your yard layout and the maximum load width.

Do you supply the rail and foundation?

SVIND supplies and installs the crane rails. Civil foundation work (concrete base and anchor bolts) is typically done by your contractor to our drawings. Erection, rail alignment, and commissioning are done by SVIND.
